# How to Read This Book

This is not a textbook to memorise from the first page to the last, and it is not a checklist waiting to be ticked. It is closer to a workshop you can return to: bring a real problem in, take a smaller action out, and carry the result back into life.

If this is your first time here, do not start with “how much should I finish”. Start with: **what do I most need to see clearly, complete, or stop right now?** That question will choose your entrance.

## Two Ways to Read, Used in Turn

### One complete reading arc

Read from beginning to end and pass through four changes:

| Stage | Reading range | What you leave with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Catch yourself | [Prologue](prologue.md) · [My Story](../part-2/my-story.md) · [Recovery](../part-2/recovery.md) | One reviewable page about the real situation: facts, costs, boundaries, and the next step |
| Learn again | [Part II: Learning Again, Opening Input](../part-1/open-input.md) and its chapters | An English baseline, one evidence card, and a training cycle you can start |
| Amplify and deliver | [Part III: Amplify Ability](../part-3/amplify-ability.md) · [Part IV: Practice and Recovery](../part-4/practice-and-recovery.md) | One real delivery, a source record, and an artifact that can be retested |
| Entrust to time | [Part V: Long-Term Action](../part-5/long-term-action.md) · [Part VI: Be Your Own Lifelong Friend](../part-6/1-understanding-yourself.md) · [Afterword](../part-6/afterword.md) | A 90-day plan, a cycle review, and a steadier relationship with yourself |

### Read it like a workshop

Jump straight to the block in front of you from the [Reading Map](../../reference/reading-map.md), the contents, or the [Glossary](../../reference/glossary.md). Stop after a section, write one judgment, do one exercise, or keep a first-version sample. Later, use the [Evidence Chain](../../templates/evidence-chain.md) and the [Weekly Review](../../templates/weekly-review.md) to check whether it transferred to a new task. You do not need to remember the whole book. You only need the next action to be clearer.

The two ways do not conflict. Reading straight through shows why the method is arranged this way; reading by need lets the method work today.

## Four Traces to Leave in Every Chapter

Do not only mark a chapter “read”. Leave at least two of these four traces:

| Trace | The question it answers | An example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A judgment | What am I more certain of than before? | “This week I track one main problem.” |
| A sample | What output proves I did it? | A recording, an email, an error list |
| A boundary | What must not be handed to a tool or to imagination? | Do not upload client data; do not turn one experience into a rule |
| A return point | When and under what conditions will I retest? | Redo the same task with a new topic in seven days |

If a chapter leaves you with only “this makes sense”, stop collecting. Rewrite it into an action that someone else can see and you can review.

## When the Plan Breaks, Come Back Here

Interruption is not an exception to this method; it is a reality the method has to survive. Open [Learning State](../../templates/learning-state.md) first and mark what is complete, what is unconfirmed, what it cost, and the next step. If your capacity has changed, return to [the minimum viable day](../part-4/daily-system.md) or the [Rhythm Ledger](../../templates/rhythm-ledger.md). Do not make the plan heavier with shame.

Add tasks back only when a repeatable rhythm has returned. A reliable system is not one that never breaks. It is one that still knows the way home after it breaks.

## One Page of Promises to the Reader

Before you turn the page, write four sentences:

1. The real problem I am handling now is:
2. The baseline evidence I already have is:
3. After this reading, I will complete this smallest action, at this time:
4. These information, privacy, health, or relationship boundaries are mine to hold:

Those four sentences are enough to begin. Reading is not leaving life behind. Reading lets life regain a direction that can be seen.

For choosing an entrance by a specific problem, preparing for an overseas interview, helping a lower-secondary learner, or checking whether you have really changed, see the full tables in the [Reading Map](../../reference/reading-map.md).
